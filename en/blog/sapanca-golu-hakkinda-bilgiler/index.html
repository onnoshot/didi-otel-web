About Lake Sapanca: Depth, Size and Can You Swim in It?

16 November 20266 min readSapanca

Lake Sapanca is a freshwater lake lying between Sakarya and Kocaeli at about 33 metres above sea level. It has a surface area of about 45 km², an east–west length of 16 km, an average depth of 36 m and a deepest point of about 52 m. The lake supplies about 90 per cent of Sakarya's drinking water and also provides water to Kocaeli. There is no single national rule on swimming in the lake; however, because it is a drinking-water source, has shores that drop away suddenly and is prone to sudden winds, we do not recommend that our guests swim in it. Below we have gathered the things people most often wonder about the lake, with sources.

Summary
  • Size: ~45 km² surface, 16 × 5 km, ~1.7 km³ of water.
  • Depth: average 36 m, deepest ~52 m (61 m in some sources).
  • Importance: ~90% of Sakarya's drinking water; drinking water for Kocaeli and process water for industry.
  • Swimming: Ask the local authority for the current rule; we do not recommend it because of the drinking-water source and safety risks.

How deep is Lake Sapanca?

The lake's average depth is about 36 metres and its deepest point about 52 metres; another section of the same source also gives a value of 61 metres. A common confusion online is that the "water level" elevations announced by SASKİ (the Sakarya water and sewerage authority), for example 31–32 m, are mistaken for depth. These values show the lake's height above sea level, in other words its elevation; they have nothing to do with depth.

How many km² is Lake Sapanca?

The lake has a surface area of about 45 km²; the long-term average is given as 46.9 km². The "251 km²" figure that appears on some sites refers not to the lake itself but to the catchment basin that feeds it (the source gives 251 and 296 km² for the basin). Routes that go around the lake by road come to 45–50 km, depending on the road chosen.

Can you swim in Lake Sapanca?

This is the most frequently asked question, and the short answer is this: the lake is the source of the water that flows from the taps of thousands of people, and it is managed under strict protection by SASKİ. Because the rules and signs about swimming on the shore can vary from area to area, you should find out the current situation from Sakarya Metropolitan Municipality and SASKİ, or from the warning signs on the shore. We do not recommend that our guests swim in the lake; the reasons:

  • Drinking-water source: Sunscreen, soap and any similar substance mixes directly into the drinking water.
  • Sudden deepening: In a lake with an average depth of 36 m, the depth can increase quickly a short distance from the shore.
  • Sudden wind: On the evening of 22 July 2026, 35 people who had gone out on the lake on SUPs (stand-up paddleboards) and boats in a sudden storm were rescued by AFAD (Turkey's disaster and emergency management authority); in July 2025, 17 people had also been rescued because of strong wind.
  • Cold water: Outside summer, the water temperature is not suitable for swimming.

If you want to cool off, in the pool season you can use our hotel's chlorine-free pool; you can enjoy the lake by walking along the shore, cycling or paddleboarding with a life jacket. Details are in our article on Lake Sapanca activities.

Why is the lake so important? Drinking water and protection

According to the information compiled on Wikipedia, Lake Sapanca supplies about 90 per cent of the city of Sakarya's drinking water, and provides drinking water to Kocaeli and process water to Tüpraş (the refinery). The drought of recent years brought the lake's water level down to the lowest levels on record; for this reason Sakarya Metropolitan Municipality and SASKİ have taken various measures:

  • Removal of illegal jetties and structures on the shore,
  • Regular shoreline clean-ups around the lake,
  • A board of scientists guiding the management of the lake,
  • Tighter checks on losses and leaks, and controlled water abstraction.

As visitors, our contribution is simple: do not leave litter on the shore, light barbecues and fires only in permitted areas, and do not put anything into the lake.

Which birds and fish live in the lake?

The lake is home to 40 fish species from 11 families. It is also important for waterbirds: the source lists the ferruginous duck (near threatened) and the white-headed duck (endangered), both with global conservation status, among the species recorded at the lake. The best times for birdwatching are the early morning hours along the reedy shores and the spring and autumn migration periods.

Why does the lake's water level change?

The level of Lake Sapanca does not stay fixed at a natural shoreline; it changes constantly with rainfall, evaporation and abstraction for drinking water. Thanks to the weir on the Çark Deresi at the lake's outlet, the level is kept within a certain range; the source gives this planned range as elevations between 29.90 and 31.50 metres. In dry years the streams that feed the lake weaken, evaporation increases and the shoreline retreats. That is why there were reports in recent years' drought that the level had fallen to the lowest on record. When reading news about the level, let us remind you once more: figures such as 31–32 metres are not the lake's depth but its height above sea level.
